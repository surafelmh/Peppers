What is the Scoville Scale?

The Scoville Scale is a tool used to measure the spiciness of chili peppers, as well as several other foods. It was created by Wilbur Scoville in 1912 while he was searching for a pepper to use as a medical ointment.

How Pepper Spice is Measured

Over time, this method of testing became outdated, and a new method using modern technology proved far more effective and efficient.

The Original Organoleptic Test

  • A dried pepper is dissolved in alcohol, effectively extracting the capsaicin oil
  • This extract is then repeatedly diluted in sugar water
  • Human taste testers repeatedly try the solution until the solution is no longer spicy
  • The final dilution number is equal to the Scoville Heat Units

High-Performance Liquid Chromatography

  • The pepper is dried, ground up, and injected into an HPLC machine
  • The machine measures the exact amount of capsaicinoids in parts per million
  • Scientists multiply the ppm by a conversion factor to directly correlate the number to traditional Scoville units

Examples of Pepper Spice Levels

The Scoville scale ranges from 0 to 2,693,000 SHU. The following table provides various, common peppers with their SHU to visualize the levels of spice.
